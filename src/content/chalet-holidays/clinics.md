## Who is it for?

You don't need to be an expert — the point is to make you better than you are now. If you can ski a blue run parallel and will tackle a red with enthusiasm, you're in. Advanced skiers wanting to master bumps and steeps, and free-riders keen to cover as much terrain as possible, are just as welcome.

We split into small groups by ability and goals. Friends and partners can ski together, but the stronger skier joins the lower group. Lots of people come alone — everyone bonds in the chalet before they've even reached the snow. And there's no age group: we've had teenagers, octogenarians and every age in between.

## A typical day

We're skiing by 9am after a short shuttle to the lifts, and break early for lunch around midday. The afternoon session runs until the lifts close at about 4pm. Later, your group meets to watch and discuss the day's video — unnerving at first, but everyone's in the same boat, and you'll quickly see what to fix. Dinner is at around 7.30pm, then a drink in town or a chat by the fire.

## Peter Hardy on the clinics

> Skiing in early December? You must be kidding — there's no snow. Yes, there is: plenty of it in Val d'Isère, on the Pisaillas glacier at 3400m and the Grande Motte at 3500m. In the third decade of running these clinics, we've always had sufficient snow cover. More often than not, we're half buried in the stuff. Pat has the uncanny ability not only to see what you're doing wrong, but to give you an easy way to put it right.
> — Peter Hardy
