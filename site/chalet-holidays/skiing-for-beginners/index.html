Skiing for beginners

Your first ski holiday

How do you choose a holiday when you don't know what a ski resort is like — let alone what to wear? Let us help. And if you'd like to know which of our chalets suit beginners best, just call 01484 548996.

  2. 2. Book a lesson

1. Get some gear

Beg and borrow (don't steal) from friends, or rent. You'll need:

  • Waterproof gloves — warm ones.
  • Salopettes — waterproof ski trousers.
  • A waterproof, windproof jacket — add warmth underneath with layers.
  • Layers — long-sleeved tops, fleeces, leggings. Proper thermals are nice but not essential.
  • Ski socks — thicker than normal, but not too thick. Take a few pairs and some travel wash.
  • A warm hat that covers your ears, goggles and a neck warmer.
  • Sun protection — proper sunglasses, high-SPF sunscreen and lip balm. Snow reflects the sun and you'll burn even when it's cold.

2. Book a lesson

Very few people teach themselves to ski. A couple of lessons at a UK indoor slope gets the basics out of the way, so you can enjoy the scenery when you arrive. Book lessons in resort too — you'll improve faster, feel more confident and reduce the risk of injury. Private lessons suit nervous starters; week-long group lessons give lots of encouragement. Book early for school holidays. See our ski school recommendations.

3. Prepare to be frustrated

For most people, skiing is hard at first. You'll ache in places you didn't know existed and get hot and sweaty. But the exhilaration when you make it down your first run without falling is incredible.

4. Fuel yourself

You'll burn a lot of energy, so eat more and drink plenty of water. One of skiing's great pleasures is a well-earned drink on a sunny terrace — and you'll genuinely notice the difference if you don't refuel.

5. Laugh

Learning to ski isn't elegant. Film it — in a couple of years, when you're cruising down black runs, you'll enjoy watching yourself snowplough at a snail's pace.

How to save money

  • Check before buying a lift pass. Beginner lifts are often free for the first day or two — ask your ski school. See lift passes.
  • Borrow, don't buy. Use what you have or what friends can lend.
  • Rent equipment. Hire shops fit boots, skis, poles and helmets — and swap boots if they pinch. See ski hire.
  • Go catered. A package with flights, transfers, most meals and wine lets you relax and keeps your spending money for lessons and lunches.
  • Get fit. Exercise beforehand, stretch and stay hydrated to cut the risk of injury. Carry a credit card on the slopes in case you need to pay up front.

Final thoughts

You'll fall over. A lot. Your children will be better than you — far better. Hang on in there: by midweek you'll be pleased with your progress. And be warned, it only takes a week to catch the bug.
